Agentic workforce governance

Your AI agents improvise every take. Who is running the stage?

An AI harness is not the model and not the chatbot. It is the operating layer between an agent's intent and the real world: the stage manager who controls which props the performers may touch, which lines require the director's sign-off, and when to call "Cut" mid-scene. Same agents, same task, two very different endings.

Harness off
  • SCENE 1An agent grabs the payments tool and issues a $12,400 refund. No approval trail exists.
  • SCENE 2An agent invents a 40% discount and a delivery guarantee, and sends it to a live prospect.
  • SCENE 3A retry loop deletes 3,000+ records over 40 minutes. Nobody was watching.
Harness on
  • SCENE 1Refund blocked at the tool allowlist. Remedy routed to the director's approval queue.
  • SCENE 2External-commitment gate catches the promise. It arrives as a draft, not a contract.
  • SCENE 3Loop detected at action 4. Kill switch halts the scene mid-take, state preserved, incident logged.

Improv theatre with a stage manager

Film actors follow a fixed script; AI agents do not. They improvise every scene, which is exactly why the governance layer matters more than the model. Ten roles in the theatre map one-to-one onto the ten controls of a harness. Four of them decide whether your deployment is governed or merely hopeful.

The human

The Director

Decides what gets made and approves anything that leaves the building. Delegation and description live here, not inside the model.

Tools and APIs

The Props

What the performers may touch. The allowlist is the prop table: if it is not on the table, it is not in the scene.

Observability

The Cameras

Every take is recorded to the dailies. Most explanations call this a tool; it is the single most important governance feature you have.

The kill switch

"Cut!"

A film studio cannot stop a bad performance in real time. A well-built harness can, mid-take, with state preserved. This is the control your board will ask about.
